# Visual notes

Salt and Light sets Christ's Sermon on the Mount from Matthew 5: the Beatitudes, salt and light, the Law fulfilled, and the call to love enemies and be perfect as the Father is perfect. The recording is a slow (64 BPM) deep-soul setting with a weathered, grave and compassionate voice. The film answers it with stillness, depth and light.

## The arc: one night to one dawn

The film passes through a single night on the hills above the Sea of Galilee and ends at sunrise.

- **Beatitudes (verse 1): deep night.** Dark, quiet places, each given one small light that answers its blessing: an empty bowl filled by a shaft of light, rain over a sheltered flame, a shoot in dark soil, a cup filling at a spring, oil poured into a lamp, still water mirroring the stars, olive leaves in moonlight, light through thorns, clouds parting on heaven.
- **Chorus: lamps and salt.** Salt crystals glittering in lamplight; lamps kindling along the shore; the city on a hill, hundreds of windows above a dark valley; the lamp lifted from under the basket onto the lampstand; light spilling out of an open door; light rising to the Father. The second chorus returns to these worlds in the blue hour before dawn, with new shots.
- **The Law fulfilled (verse 2): stone and lamplight.** The scroll and its letters filling with light, the smallest letter that will not pass away, embers of anger, the gift left at the altar, the road out to make peace and back, incense rising clean.
- **The heart (verse 3): the inner lamp.** A guttering lamp, the pruned vine, the far valley of Gehenna's fires, a plumb line hanging true for yes and no.
- **The other way (bridge): roads in the grey-blue before dawn.** The balance of an eye for an eye, the turn from shadow into light, a cloak given, the milestone and the second mile, a door opened to the one who asks.
- **Love your enemies (outro): sunrise.** Two fires across a valley, a vigil lamp and prayer rope, the sun rising on fields good and bad, rain on just and unjust, and the mount in full dawn for "Be perfect".

## Look

Dark, quiet frames with light breaking in. Light is never decoration: it is always the meaning of the shot. Shadows are deep but never crushed; every frame has a lit subject and real depth (lens focus and bokeh, haze, soft shadows). Something moves at every moment: flame, water, smoke, mist, wind, or the camera.

Palette: night indigo and warm black; lamp amber; salt silver-white with small prismatic glints; the Father's gold; dawn rose and gold. Cold blue-grey only for anger, sin and judgment.

No human figures in full. People appear only as distant silhouettes, shadows, or small details (a footprint, a cloak, a lamp set down). No faces. Christ is present as the speaking voice and as light, never as a figure.

Theology follows the Orthodox reading of the text: the blessings are the Kingdom given now and fulfilled in heaven; the Law is fulfilled, not abolished; the light is the Father's glory shining through good works; "be perfect" is the call to be made like God, shown as light filling everything rather than as human achievement.

## Words

The words are the voice of Christ, so they are set with gravity: EB Garamond for the teaching, tall capitals (Bebas Neue) for the few words that carry the weight of a line. Each word arrives on its sung onset and is sharp and readable over a dark area of the picture, never on a bright sky. Colour follows meaning:

- gold, lit: blessed, kingdom, heaven, God, Father, glory, perfect
- salt white with a cold glint: salt, earth (in the chorus)
- warm lamp amber, lit: light, lamp, shine, city, house
- soft rose italic: mercy, peace, comfort, love, gentle, pure
- cold ash capitals: murder, anger, judgment, sin, hell, lust, enemy
- clear white capitals: yes, no

Layouts vary with the picture and the meaning of each line: position, size and grouping answer the composition. At most two lines at once.
