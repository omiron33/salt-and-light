# Storyboard: salt-and-light

Written before any animation. One row per stretch of the song. Read the lyric column top to bottom with the sound off: the story should still come through.

| Time | On screen | What the moment is for | How it leaves | What carries into the next shot |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0:00.0–0:16.8 | Title. In the first second the camera is already gliding low over the black Sea of Galilee at night, faint stars mirrored on slow swells; ahead the dark ridge of the mount. A single clay oil lamp sits on a rock at the water's edge in the foreground; its wick catches and the flame leaps up, warm light spreading over wet stones. SALT AND LIGHT sets in tall pale capitals, MATTHEW 5 small beneath, in the dark sky; the camera keeps rising past the lamp toward the mount. | We are on the hillside by the sea at night, and one light has been lit. | The camera rises over the lamp into darkness above the hill. | The lamp's warm light, night. |
| 0:16.8–0:24.4 | Lyric: “Blessed are the poor in spirit” “Heaven's kingdom belongs to them”. Inside a dark stone room: an empty, worn wooden bowl on a rough table, dust in the air. A narrow shaft of pale gold light descends from a gap in the roof and slowly fills the empty bowl until it glows, motes turning in the beam. | The poor in spirit: empty, and given the Kingdom. | The beam brightens; slow push into the glowing bowl. | Gold light in a vessel. |
| 0:24.4–0:32.0 | Lyric: “Blessed are the ones who mourn” “They will find their comfort”. Night rain falling on dark basalt stones by a doorway; drops strike and splash; behind, through the rain and out of focus, a lamp burns steady in a sheltered niche. On 'comfort' the rain eases and the drops in front of the lamp catch its warm light like falling sparks. | Those who mourn are met by comfort. | Rack focus from the rain to the steady lamp. | Water and warm light. |
| 0:32.0–0:38.2 | Lyric: “Blessed are the gentle” “They will inherit the earth”. Macro on dark tilled soil at night, in moonlight: a green shoot uncurls from the earth, a dewdrop on its leaf. On 'inherit the earth' the camera rises and the field opens wide: furrowed earth running to the hills, the moonlit horizon. | The gentle inherit the earth: small and low, then given everything. | The camera keeps rising toward the moonlit hills. | Earth under the moon. |
| 0:38.2–0:45.4 | Lyric: “Those who hunger and thirst for righteousness” “Will be filled”. Cracked dry ground at night under a dark sky; a thin stream of water finds the cracks and runs into a clay cup set in the earth; the cup fills, brims and overflows, the water lit gold from within on 'filled'. | Hunger and thirst for righteousness, and the filling. | The overflow spreads over the ground toward the lens. | Overflowing water. |
| 0:45.4–0:49.7 | Lyric: “Mercy will meet the merciful”. Close and warm: golden olive oil poured from a small clay jar into a dark, empty lamp; the oil fills the bowl and soaks the wick; a flame rises on 'merciful'. | Mercy given is mercy received: oil poured into the lamp. | The new flame flickers and settles. | A small flame. |
| 0:49.7–0:53.0 | Lyric: “Pure hearts will see God”. A perfectly still black lake at night mirroring the stars; the last ripple settles to glass, and in the reflection a great soft light opens in the heavens on 'God'. | The pure in heart will see God: stillness becomes vision. | The light in the reflection swells. | Stillness and silver light. |
| 0:53.0–1:01.1 | Lyric: “Those who make peace will be called God's children”. An olive grove in moonlight: gnarled trunks and silver leaves stirring in the wind; the camera glides slowly between the trees; two long moon-cast human shadows (no figures in frame) approach from opposite sides, their shadow hands clasp, and moonlight breaks into a clearing on 'children'. | The peacemakers: quiet and patient, called God's children. | The camera drifts into the moonlit clearing. | Silver light through leaves. |
| 1:01.1–1:04.9 | Lyric: “Those who suffer for doing right have the kingdom”. Dark thorn branches in silhouette close to the lens; behind them a band of warm light rises; it breaks through the gaps between the thorns on 'kingdom'. | Suffering for what is right, and the Kingdom that shines through it. | The light flares through the thorns. | Light through dark shapes. |
| 1:04.9–1:09.2 | Lyric: “When they speak against you because of Me”. Storm clouds rolling low over the dark hills, wind driving dust and grass; far below a single tiny lamp holds against the wind. | When they speak against you: the storm, and the light that holds. | The camera tilts up into the clouds. | Clouds overhead. |
| 1:09.2–1:15.9 | Lyric: “Rejoice; your reward is in heaven”. The camera rises through the storm clouds, which part into a vast clear night of stars; light pours down through the opening on 'heaven'. | Rejoice: the reward is in heaven. | The opening widens into light. | Stars and light from above. |
| 1:15.9–1:19.7 | Lyric: “You are the salt of the earth”. Extreme macro of salt: clear cubic crystals glittering, catching lamplight with small prismatic glints as the lens glides across them and the focus breathes. | You are the salt of the earth: small, bright, precious. | The camera pulls back from the crystals. | Glittering white. |
| 1:19.7–1:23.5 | Lyric: “Do not let it lose its taste”. A white salt pan on the shore of the Dead Sea at night under the moon: crusted crystal shapes running to dark water; in the foreground one patch of salt is dull grey while the rest still glints. | Do not lose your saltiness. | The camera glides over the glinting crust. | Moonlit white. |
| 1:23.5–1:27.4 | Lyric: “You are the light for the world”. The shore of the Sea of Galilee at night: lamps kindle one after another along the dark shore and the hills, until the darkness is strung with points of warm light. | You are the light of the world. | The lights keep spreading up the hill. | Many lamps on a hill. |
| 1:27.4–1:31.2 | Lyric: “A city on a hill cannot hide”. The hero shot: a hilltop town at night above a misty valley, hundreds of warm windows and lamps glowing on the stone houses; the camera rises slowly from the valley floor toward it. | A city on a hill cannot be hidden. | The camera keeps rising toward the town. | Warm windows in stone. |
| 1:31.2–1:35.0 | Lyric: “No one lights a lamp to cover it”. Inside a dark stone house: a clay lamp under an upturned woven basket, light leaking through the weave in glowing points; the basket lifts away and the flame stands free. | No one lights a lamp to hide it. | The flame stands free and brightens. | The free flame. |
| 1:35.0–1:39.3 | Lyric: “Set it where the whole house can see”. The lamp set on a tall lampstand; its light spreads through the whole house: arches, water jars, bread on the table, the room revealed in warm light. | Set it where the whole house can see. | The camera glides through the lit room. | Warm light filling a room. |
| 1:39.3–1:44.5 | Lyric: “Let your good works shine before others”. An open doorway at night: warm light spills from the house onto the stone street and the steps; the long shadow of a person carries a loaf out of the light and across the street to a dark neighbouring door; on the wall the two shadows' hands pass the loaf from one to the other, and that door opens and lights (shadows only). | Let your good works shine before others. | Push out through the door into the street. | Light spilling outward. |
| 1:44.5–1:49.9 | Lyric: “And give your Father glory”. The camera rises from the lit doorways of the town up into the sky; sparks and light rise with it toward a vast field of stars and a gold glow on 'glory'. | Give glory to your Father in heaven. | The rise continues into the night sky. | Stars, gold glow. |
| 1:49.9–2:02.2 | Instrumental. A slow glide low over the Sea of Galilee at night: a small fishing boat with a lantern at its prow rests on still water; stars overhead; mist drifts; the lantern's reflection trembles; the first deep blue begins along the eastern hills. | A breath: the night sea, the teaching heard in quiet. | The camera drifts past the boat toward the shore. | Lantern light on water. |
| 2:02.2–2:09.8 | Lyric: “Do not think I came to tear down the Law” “Or the words the prophets gave”. A Torah scroll open on a wooden lectern in a dark synagogue of stone; a lamp beside it lights the columns of letters; slow glide along the parchment. | The Law and the Prophets are not torn down. | The camera glides along the text. | Lamplit letters. |
| 2:09.8–2:13.6 | Lyric: “I came to bring them to fulfillment”. The letters of the scroll fill with gold light, one column after another, as if from within. | Fulfilled: the Law filled with light. | The gold glow spreads off the page. | Gold letters. |
| 2:13.6–2:20.8 | Lyric: “Not one small mark will fall away” “Before all is accomplished”. Extreme macro on one tiny letter (a yod) on the parchment; dust drifts and falls across the page but the small mark stays, glowing faintly. | Not the smallest mark will pass away. | The focus holds on the mark as the dust settles. | One small mark. |
| 2:20.8–2:25.0 | Lyric: “You heard the command against murder”. Match cut: the yod's shape carved on a rough stone; pull back to a dark stone field under a cold night sky, the stone lying in cold light, a dark stain of shadow beside it. | The command against murder. | The camera lowers toward the stone. | Cold stone. |
| 2:25.0–2:28.8 | Lyric: “I tell you anger also faces judgment”. Embers in the dark: coals smoulder, then flare angry red and spit sparks on 'anger', then a cold white light falls on them on 'judgment'. | Anger, too, faces judgment. | The embers dim under the cold light. | Red embers. |
| 2:28.8–2:32.1 | Lyric: “When you bring your gift to the altar”. A stone altar in a temple court at night; a basket with loaves and a jar of oil set down on its step in lamplight; smoke from the altar fire rises. | Bringing your gift to the altar. | The camera rises with the smoke. | The gift on the step. |
| 2:32.1–2:36.5 | Lyric: “And remember your brother's grievance”. A deep crack in a stone pavement, two long shadows on either side of it, apart. | Remember your brother's grievance. | The crack runs away from the lens. | The divide. |
| 2:36.5–2:41.7 | Lyric: “Leave your gift and go make peace” “Then come and offer it”. The gift left on the altar step; the camera moves out through the gate onto a road in the blue hour, footprints in the dust leading away; the two separated shadows meet on the road and embrace; then back: the gift is offered and clean incense smoke rises into gold light. | Go and make peace, then come and offer it. | The smoke rises straight into the light. | Rising incense, gold. |
| 2:41.7–2:45.5 | Lyric: “You are the salt of the earth”. Salt in the blue hour: rough salt crystals on dark stone by the water, the first cold blue light catching their edges; slow glide. | The chorus returns: salt of the earth, now before dawn. | Pull back from the crystals. | Blue and white glints. |
| 2:45.5–2:49.3 | Lyric: “Do not let it lose its taste”. A bowl of coarse salt beside broken bread on a rough table, lit by a lamp; the camera's focus slides across the salt grains. | Do not let it lose its taste: salt that seasons. | Rack focus to the bread. | Lamp, bread, salt. |
| 2:49.3–2:53.2 | Lyric: “You are the light for the world”. The hills above the sea in the blue hour: a line of first light along the eastern ridge; small lamps still lit on the slopes below. | You are the light of the world, as the dawn begins. | The camera pans along the ridge. | First light. |
| 2:53.2–2:57.0 | Lyric: “A city on a hill cannot hide”. The city on the hill from high above in the blue hour: its windows still lit, mist in the valleys, the sea beyond. | A city on a hill cannot hide, seen anew. | The camera descends toward the town. | Warm windows in blue. |
| 2:57.0–3:00.8 | Lyric: “No one lights a lamp to cover it”. A lamp in a wall niche covered by a cloth; the cloth slides away and the light spills across the stone room. | No one covers a lamp. | The light spreads across the wall. | The uncovered lamp. |
| 3:00.8–3:05.1 | Lyric: “Set it where the whole house can see”. From outside: a stone house whose windows light one by one, room after room, until the whole house glows. | The whole house can see. | The camera pulls back from the house. | A glowing house. |
| 3:05.1–3:10.3 | Lyric: “Let your good works shine before others”. A lit doorway at dawn; the shadow of a person carries a lit lamp from the first doorway to the next and lights it, and the light is carried on down the lane (shadows only). | Good works shine before others and spread. | The lights run down the lane. | A lane of lamps. |
| 3:10.3–3:17.7 | Lyric: “And give your Father glory”. The camera rises over the town into the deepest blue hour, a faint pale band low in the east; one star fading; light gathers on 'glory'. | Give your Father glory. | The sky brightens toward dawn. | Deep blue, first pale band. |
| 3:17.7–3:25.3 | Lyric: “You heard the command against adultery” “I tell you lust can sin within the heart”. A dark room; a clay lamp's flame gutters and smokes black (the lamp of the body); on 'heart' the light shrinks to a dim, unsteady glow. | Lust within the heart: the inner light darkened. | The smoke curls up into darkness. | A smoking flame. |
| 3:25.3–3:31.7 | Lyric: “If your right eye leads you into sin” “Tear it out and throw it away”. A vine in the dark, lit from the side; a pruning knife's blade (no hand visible) severs a withered branch, which falls away into darkness. | Better to cut away what leads you into sin. | The cut branch falls out of frame. | The pruned vine. |
| 3:31.7–3:38.9 | Lyric: “Better to lose one part of you” “Than your whole body be cast into hell”. Far below, the Valley of Hinnom at night: low refuse fires smouldering red in a deep ravine, smoke rising, seen from the dark rim under the cold grey-blue before dawn. | The warning of Gehenna. | The camera pulls back from the rim. | Red smoke in the dark. |
| 3:38.9–3:43.2 | Lyric: “You heard the command against false vows”. A stone wall with carved Hebrew oath marks; cold light; a plumb line hangs before it, swinging. | False vows. | The plumb line slows. | The swinging line. |
| 3:43.2–3:48.9 | Lyric: “But let your yes be yes” “And let your no be no”. The plumb line comes to rest, perfectly true, in clean lamplight; the stone behind is straight. | Let your yes be yes and your no be no. | Hold on the true line. | A straight line of light. |
| 3:48.9–3:55.7 | Lyric: “You heard an eye for an eye” “But I tell you, turn the other cheek”. A pair of bronze balance scales swinging in cold light; then the camera turns slowly around a stone pillar from its shadowed side into warm light on 'turn the other cheek'. | An eye for an eye, answered by turning. | The turn ends in the warm light. | Warm light on stone. |
| 3:55.7–4:01.8 | Lyric: “If someone takes your coat” “Give your cloak as well”. A woven wool cloak lying on stones by a road in the grey-blue before dawn, its folds moving in the wind; a second cloak is laid over it. | Give your cloak as well. | The wind lifts the fabric. | Folds of wool. |
| 4:01.8–4:09.0 | Lyric: “If you are forced to walk one mile” “Go with them another”. A Roman milestone beside a road in the grey-blue before dawn; the road runs past it and on toward the band of coming dawn in the east; a second milestone far ahead; footprints continue past the first. | Go the second mile. | The camera glides on down the road. | The road into light. |
| 4:09.0–4:16.6 | Lyric: “Give to the one who asks” “Do not turn away the one who needs to borrow”. In the grey-blue before dawn a door opens outward; warm light spills across the threshold where a loaf and a few coins are set down; the door stays open. | Give to the one who asks; do not turn away. | The door's light spreads into the street. | The open door. |
| 4:16.6–4:20.7 | Lyric: “You heard, love your neighbor and hate your enemy”. Two campfires on opposite hills in the deep blue just before dawn, across a dark valley, each alone. | Love your neighbor and hate your enemy: the divide. | The camera drifts between the fires. | Two fires. |
| 4:20.7–4:24.2 | Lyric: “But I tell you, love your enemies”. A thread of light runs across the valley from one fire to the other and joins them. | Love your enemies. | The joined light brightens. | The bridge of light. |
| 4:24.2–4:28.5 | Lyric: “Pray for the ones who persecute you”. A small red vigil lamp (lampada) glowing in a dark corner; beside it a black wool prayer rope; the flame trembles and steadies. | Pray for those who persecute you. | Slow push into the lamp's glow. | Red glow, prayer. |
| 4:28.5–4:32.3 | Lyric: “Your Father sends sunlight on evil and good”. Sunrise over the hills of Galilee: the first sun strikes and sweeps across the land, over a field of wheat and a field of thorns alike. | The sun rises on evil and good. | The sunlight sweeps on across the land. | Gold sunlight. |
| 4:32.3–4:36.4 | Lyric: “And rain on the just and unjust”. Rain falls in sunlight across the hills: glittering drops on both fields, a faint rainbow in the mist. | Rain on the just and the unjust. | The rain thins. | Bright rain. |
| 4:36.4–4:43.8 | Lyric: “If you love only those who love you” “What reward is there?”. A lone lamp in a small window at dawn, then a pull back that shows it is one of very few; a quiet pause on 'What reward is there?' | If you love only those who love you, what reward? | The camera keeps pulling back into the wide morning. | One small light. |
| 4:43.8–4:55.9 | Lyric: “Be perfect, as your Father in heaven is perfect”. The mount above the Sea of Galilee at full dawn: light pours over the hills and across the water; the camera rises slowly as the whole landscape fills with gold light on 'perfect'. | Be perfect as your Father in heaven is perfect. | The rise continues into the bright sky. | The full light of morning. |
| 4:55.9–5:06.8 | End title over the dawn sea: SALT AND LIGHT, Matthew 5:3–48, the light settling; the lamp from the opening burns on the rock in daylight, small and steady; fade to black at the end of the music. | Rest: the teaching given, the light kept. | Fade to black. | End. |
